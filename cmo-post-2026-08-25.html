I once met an owner who had never seen his real books. He ran a construction company. He'd handed all of it to his secretary. She was entering transactions into an outdated system. Every day. For years. She didn't know what the numbers meant. Neither did he. He thought the business was doing well because the bank account looked healthy. Nobody had ever shown him a proper report. This is what stayed with me. He wasn't careless. He'd simply trusted someone who was never trained to know what she was looking at. That gap can sit quietly for years before anyone notices it. We came in and started from the ground up. Taught her how to record transactions properly. Went through their supporting documents in person, not just the spreadsheet. Then taught them both how to actually read what the reports were saying. Within a few years, that company grew from 8 people to more than 50. I think the real risk in most SME accounting is someone entering numbers they don't understand, while the owner never asks to see what those numbers actually mean. We think like owners, so we check the books the way the owner would, if the owner actually knew what to look for. Save this for the situation where you find out your books were wrong from someone else, not from you. P.S. Untrained hands cost more than bad intentions ever do.
