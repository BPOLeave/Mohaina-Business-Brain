## Mohaina Asira

---

## SECTION 1: ORIGIN & FOUNDATION

### The Moment That Changed Everything

A client finally got clarity about the performance of his company — because of the reports, the proper accounting and updated records we put in place. Before that, he believed the cash sitting in his bank account was the measure of how his business was doing. But the real picture was different. He had significant accounts receivable waiting to be collected. He was making good sales. He just couldn't see it.

That moment — watching a business owner understand his own numbers for the first time — is the clearest picture of what this work actually does.

### The Person Who Shaped Them

Her manager at Travelstart. Young, capable, country manager level — strong at running the business, generating sales, all of it. But not good at handling people. He had good intentions, they just didn't land right. The team couldn't feel the intention behind the decision. They resented him for it instead of following him.

Mohaina kept finding herself in the middle. Translating. Explaining what he actually meant. Bridging the gap between what was intended and what was heard.

That experience quietly shaped how she leads. She learned early that intention without delivery costs you the room.

### The Value Running Their Decisions

*"Never let someone who betrayed you have another opportunity to do it to you again."*

It's a protection principle, and it runs quietly beneath every client relationship, every hire, every agreement she enters. She's not harsh with it — but she doesn't test it twice.

---

## SECTION 2: CHARACTER UNDER PRESSURE

### The Hardest Chapter

When she first joined BPOAS as an owner, it was on the edge of collapse. Salaries delayed. Invoices not collected. No office. Visas getting cancelled. Some staff with no working visa at all.

She juggled the budget through all of it. Maxed out most of her credit cards. Exhausted her savings. It strained her marriage.

She didn't talk about what she had to become. But the fact that she held it together — and held on — says what words didn't.

### The Risk That Defined Them

Leaving everything she knew and moving to Dubai.

She had worked away from home before — four years in Manila after growing up in Mindanao. But that was still the Philippines. This was a different country, different culture, no contacts, no network, no one she knew.

She took the leap anyway. She's clear she doesn't count herself successful yet. But she measures the distance from where she started — and she's helped her family back home along the way.

That counts for something.

### The Mistake That Built Them

A reconstruction job. They quoted AED 18,000 to rebuild three years of accounts from scratch. Without scoping the work properly first.

Then they had to do it. From scratch. Every transaction, every period, every correction. It drained time and nearly all available resources.

The lesson wasn't to say no to difficult jobs. It was to scope the work before you price it. Every time, without exception.

### Standing Their Ground

Some clients breached the non-solicitation clause — not directly, but creatively. A team member would resign, wait a period, then quietly start working for the client. Not something that stays hidden forever.

She refused to work with those clients again.

And she's refused to work with clients who treated her employees rudely. No engagement is worth that. People don't get paid enough to absorb poor behaviour. She'd make the same call again.

---

## SECTION 3: THE WORK & THE WHY

### When They Knew

She's still not entirely certain. That honesty itself is worth noting.

But the clearest signal she describes is the moment a client understands something. Gets clarity. Avoids a cost they wouldn't have seen coming. That's when she feels it — something that could be called purpose, even if she doesn't name it that way.

*"Every time the client is happy because they got clarity, or they avoided cost because of what we did, it's something that makes me proud."*

### What They Do Differently

They think like owners.

Not as external service providers hired to tick compliance boxes — as people who feel every dirham lost as if it came from their own pocket.

The physical proof: they go to the client's office. They check supporting documents in person. They don't just accept a spreadsheet or a data extract and build a VAT return from it. They look at the actual invoices and bills. Because only then can you see whether what's written is FTA-compliant, whether the input tax is actually claimable, whether the documentation holds up.

That's the difference between doing accounting and doing it right.

### The Reframe That Changes Everything

Put yourself in the other person's shoes before you judge them.

She doesn't frame this as a big idea — she describes it almost quietly, as something she does reflexively before she reacts. But it's the operating principle beneath how she manages difficult clients, how she interpreted her manager at Travelstart, how she holds her team.

Understanding first. Judgment after, if at all.

### The Work That Made Them Proud

A construction company. The owner had delegated everything to a secretary who was entering transactions into an outdated system — without understanding what reports meant, what they showed, or what proper accounting structure looked like.

Mohaina came in and trained them from the ground up. How to record transactions properly. How to generate meaningful reports. How to change the accounting system. How to read what the numbers were actually saying.

The company went from 8 employees to more than 50.

---

## SECTION 4: WORLDVIEW & DIRECTION

### What Everyone Gets Wrong

Two things. And she sees both repeatedly.

The first is complacency — the assumption that because you've done the work, it's right. In accounting and tax compliance, that assumption costs clients real money. A second set of eyes isn't bureaucratic process. It's protection. Once a submission goes to the FTA portal, you can't change it. Penalties follow.

The second is the undervaluing of time. Not just billing for it — understanding what it means when you give it. *"You're not just giving your work or your service. You're also giving up your time."* She believes that deeply, and she sees people in her field forget it regularly.

### How Success Changed Shape

She used to think it was money. Clear, measurable, easy to track.

Now she thinks it's contentment. If someone is happy with what they have and where they are, she considers them successful — regardless of the number in their account. The evidence she cites: people with everything, still not happy. People on a farm, content with what they have, clearly at peace.

*"At the end of the day, it's how you feel within yourself that makes you successful."*

### Where This Is All Going

She sees the regulatory landscape in the UAE working in BPOAS's favour. E-invoicing mandates. Corporate income tax implementation. DMTT and Pillar Two incoming. The more compliance requirements exist, the more companies need proper accounting records and systems.

Her read: more potential clients, not fewer. Companies that previously got by without structured accounting will no longer be able to.

### What People Actually Get

Clarity on what their numbers are telling them.

And efficiency. Less manual work. Fewer hours spent on tasks that previously required multiple people. The transformation she describes isn't emotional — it's operational. Clients leave with systems that work, reports that mean something, and time back.
