Today's LinkedIn Post · Copy-Ready

HOOK — "A client had AED 800,000 sitting in receivables. His bank balance never showed it."
A client in construction showed me his bank balance last year and said business was good. I think what he didn't know was sitting right under it. He had AED 800,000 in accounts receivable that nobody had chased. On paper, cash felt fine. In reality, he was funding the business out of money that was never actually his to spend yet. Your bank balance tells you what is in the account today. It does not tell you what you are owed, what you owe, or what is coming due. That is what the numbers are actually telling you, if anyone bothers to look. Three numbers I ask every owner to check before their bank balance: What is sitting in receivables, and how old is it. Money you have not collected is not profit. It is a decision you have not made yet. What is sitting in payables, and when it is due. A healthy bank balance today can disappear the moment three invoices land at once. What your P&L says for the month, separate from what moved through the account. Cash timing and profit are different stories, and owners who read only one get surprised by the other. We go through this with every client before we touch a VAT return or a CIT filing, because a report built on the wrong story is not worth submitting. At the end of the day, the bank balance is the easiest number to check and the least reliable one to trust. Save this for the next time your bank balance looks better than your business feels. P.S. The AED 800,000 was recovered. It just took someone actually looking for it.
