Step 2 — Full Post · Copy-Ready

A good accountant should make you uncomfortable sometimes. If yours never does, that's not a good sign. I think most owners want an accountant who agrees with them. Someone who says the numbers are fine, the filing is done, don't worry about it. That feels good in the moment. It costs a lot later. We took over a client's books a few months back. Trading company, decent size. Their previous accountant had told them everything was in order for almost two years. When we opened the file, the VAT input claims didn't match the supporting documents. Nobody had checked the documents themselves, just the numbers on the return. The client wasn't angry about the mistake. He was angry that nobody told him it might be a problem before it became one. He'd been paying for peace of mind and getting silence instead. That's the actual job. Not just filing on time. Telling an owner what they don't want to hear, before the FTA is the one telling them. A cheap accountant who never pushes back, never asks a hard question, never says "this doesn't look right," is not saving you money. They're just delaying the bill, and adding interest to it. We think like owners, so when something in the books doesn't sit right, we say so, even if it's not what the client wants to hear that week. If a client loses a dirham, it's something that also comes from our pocket. That only works if we're honest about what we find. Tag a founder who needs to see this.
