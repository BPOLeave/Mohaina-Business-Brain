A trading company’s bank asked for a full year of VAT returns before approving their credit line increase. That is when the gap surfaced. Their bookkeeper had been claiming input tax on shipping and clearance costs for two years. Some of it had no valid tax invoice behind it, just a supplier statement. The bookkeeper called it handled. Nobody had checked what “handled” actually meant. We went through the returns line by line against the actual invoices, the way we do with every new client. The claimable amount and the claimed amount did not match. AED 34,000 in input tax had been claimed without anything the FTA would accept as proof. I think most owners hear “VAT is filed” and assume that is the same as “VAT is correct.” It is not. Filed on time and filed right are two different things, and only one of them protects you when the FTA asks questions. We corrected two years of returns and filed the voluntary disclosure before the bank ever saw a red flag. The loan went through. The exposure did not carry into it. If a client loses a dirham to something we should have caught, that comes from our side too. That’s why we check the documents, not just the numbers on the return. Save this for the situation where your bookkeeper says “it’s handled.” Ask what they actually checked. P.S. A bank loan application is not the only thing that pulls your VAT history. An FTA audit does the same thing, just without the warning.

Pinned 1 — Extend (Compliance)Under UAE VAT law, input tax is only recoverable when you hold a valid tax invoice meeting the FTA’s format requirements; a supplier statement or delivery note is not enough on its own. If input tax was claimed without that, the correct route is a Voluntary Disclosure to the FTA, not a quiet adjustment on the next return. Filing it voluntarily before the FTA finds it changes the outcome significantly.
