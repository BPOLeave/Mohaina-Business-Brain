I think most owners check one number to know if the business is doing well. It's the wrong number. The bank balance answers one question: how much cash is sitting there today. It doesn't tell you what you owe, what's owed to you, or what you actually own. Here's what your balance sheet shows that your bank balance never will. 1. What you actually owe. Loans, supplier invoices, VAT payable, end of service liabilities. A healthy bank balance can sit right next to a bigger pile of obligations nobody's tracking. 2. What's owed to you. Accounts receivable sitting uncollected doesn't turn into cash until someone actually pays it. Some owners look profitable on paper and still can't cover next month's payroll. 3. What you actually own. Inventory, equipment, prepaid expenses. Real value that never touches your bank account until it's sold or used up. We had a client whose bank balance looked fine for months straight. His balance sheet told a different story: receivables piling up, a supplier facility close to its limit. The bank balance never would have shown him that. A clean balance sheet gives you the full picture. A bank balance gives you one snapshot, and only for today. Save this for your next board or bank conversation. P.S. If you can't remember the last time you actually looked at your balance sheet instead of your banking app, that's worth asking yourself why.
