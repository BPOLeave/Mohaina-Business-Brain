Most owners think their accountant's job is to keep them out of trouble. That's not the job. The job is to tell you things you don't want to hear before the FTA does. I had a client last year whose bookkeeper never once flagged an issue in two years. Every report came back clean. He was proud of that. We came in for his CIT registration review and found three years of input VAT claimed on expenses that were never actually claimable. Nobody had told him. Not because his bookkeeper was careless. Because she thought her job was to keep the peace, not to protect him. I think that's the real cost of cheap accounting. It isn't the fee you paid. It's the conversation nobody had with you. We tell clients what's wrong before we tell them what's fine. If a return needs correcting, we say so, even when it's an uncomfortable call to make. We think like owners here. If a client loses a dirham because we stayed quiet, that comes from our pocket too. Compliance isn't a box you tick once a year. It's a conversation you should be having every month, with someone willing to tell you the truth. If your accountant has never told you something you didn't want to hear, ask yourself why. DM me "FTA" and I'll tell you the three questions I'd ask your current accountant. P.S. A quiet accountant is not a good sign. It usually means nobody is actually checking.
