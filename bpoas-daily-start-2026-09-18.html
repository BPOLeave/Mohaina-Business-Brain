Pillar: What the Numbers Are Telling You · Educating · Framework List · No compliance flag
I think most owners look at their P&L once a month, see whether the number at the bottom is positive, and move on. That works until it doesn't. There are three numbers your P&L generates every month that most people never look at, and they're the ones that usually explain why things feel wrong even when the profit looks fine. 1. Gross profit margin by revenue line, not just overall. Your total gross margin can look healthy while one product or service is quietly bleeding. The blended number masks it. If you sell two or three things, you need to see the margin on each one separately, every month. 2. The gap between your invoiced revenue and your collected revenue. Your P&L shows revenue when it's billed, not when it lands in your account. A company can be technically profitable and genuinely cash-short at the same time. That gap is the first thing a bank or investor will ask about. 3. Fixed costs as a percentage of revenue, not as a flat number. When revenue dips 15%, your fixed costs don't. The percentage goes up fast. Owners who track the flat number think they know their exposure. Owners who track the percentage see the risk before it becomes a problem. None of this requires a finance degree. It just requires someone to set it up and show you where to look. Save this for your next board or bank conversation. P.S. If your accountant is not showing you these three on a monthly basis, that is worth raising at your next review.
3 PINNED COMMENTS

1. The number that surprises people most is usually the fixed-cost percentage. Most owners think their cost base is manageable until revenue drops and they watch it jump.

2. I have had this conversation in a bank meeting where the owner could not explain the gap between their profit and their cash balance. The bank could. It is a better meeting when you know the number before they ask.

3. If your accountant is not showing you these three on a monthly basis, that is worth asking about at your next review.
