Contrarian Text Post · Pillar: The True Cost of Cheap Accounting · 4/4 Invisibility Diagnostic PASS · 1,343 characters

Bookkeeping and accounting are not the same job. Most owners never find out the difference until it costs them.
I think a lot of business owners hand their books to whoever is available, not whoever is qualified. A secretary. A relative. Someone who knows Excel. It looks efficient. It isn't the same as knowing what the numbers mean. We picked up a construction company where the owner had handed everything to his secretary. She entered transactions into an outdated system every month. She kept the ledger moving. But she couldn't tell you what the reports actually showed, because nobody had ever taught her what to look for. The owner didn't know either. He was running a growing business without a real view of its own performance. We came in and trained the team from the ground up. How to record properly. How to generate reports that mean something. How to read what those reports were actually telling them. That company has grown from 8 employees to more than 50 since. I think the real risk of cheap accounting isn't a missed deadline or a wrong number. It's an owner who thinks his books are handled, when nobody in the building actually understands what they say. At the end of the day, if nobody around you can read the numbers, you're not running the business. You're guessing at it. Tag a founder who needs to see this.
