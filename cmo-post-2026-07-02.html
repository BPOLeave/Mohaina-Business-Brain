LinkedIn Post · Contrarian Text Post

Your accountant has done everything right. Your VAT is filed. Your CIT is recorded. Your books balance. So why do you still not know if your business is actually making money? This is what most UAE SME owners cannot answer. And almost no one is saying it out loud. Clean books and useful books are not the same thing. I think the confusion starts early. We are all told that good accounting means getting the numbers right. And yes, that matters. If your records are wrong, the FTA has questions. We know. But I have sat with business owners whose books were spotless. Correct VAT entries. Accurate payroll. Well-maintained ledgers. And they had no idea which product lines were losing money. They had not looked at a cash flow report in months. They were checking their bank balance to decide if they could make payroll. One owner told me he thought the business was doing fine. His gross margin had been shrinking for two years. Nobody told him. The numbers were right. No one was reading them to make decisions. There is a difference between an accountant who files your returns and one who tells you what the numbers are telling you. The first keeps you compliant. The second helps you run a business. UAE SMEs deserve the second kind. Most are getting the first. If your accountant only tells you what happened last quarter and not what to do about it, the books are clean. But the business is flying blind. Tag a founder who needs to see this.

Comment 1 — Extend: UAE compliance reference
Under the UAE Corporate Tax Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022), every taxable entity must maintain financial records that give a true and fair view of their financial position — not just records that satisfy a VAT return. The FTA can request supporting documents and management accounts during a tax audit. Clean books may satisfy a bookkeeping checklist. They do not automatically satisfy an auditor asking what your gross margin looks like and how you calculated taxable income. Two different standards. Worth knowing before the September 30 CIT filing deadline.
