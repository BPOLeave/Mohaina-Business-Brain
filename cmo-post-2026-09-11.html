There are three numbers on your P&L most owners have never actually looked at.

I think most owners open their P&L, see the bottom line, and close it again. That's the number banks want. It's not the number that tells you how the business is actually doing.

Here are three we check first, before the bottom line even matters.

1. Gross margin by product or service line, not the blended average. We had a trading client who looked profitable overall, until we split it out. One line was losing money every month. It was hiding behind two others that were carrying it.

2. Accounts receivable ageing. Not just how much is owed, how long it's been owed. Your P&L can look fine while your actual cash is stuck in invoices nobody has chased in ninety days.

3. Owner and related-party expenses running through the business. They sit quietly inside your costs and bring your real profit down, and most owners have never separated them out to see the true number.

None of this shows up when you're just watching your bank balance. That's the visibility gap that gets owners in trouble. Not because they're careless. Because a healthy balance feels like proof, and it isn't.

We think like owners here, so we build reports that show these three numbers every month, not just the bottom line.

Save this for your next board or bank conversation.

P.S. A good bank balance and a good business are not always the same thing.
