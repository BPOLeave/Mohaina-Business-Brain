Filing on time doesn't mean you're compliant. Here's what the FTA actually checks. The VAT return deadline is 28 August. I think most owners are focused on the date and not on what happens after they hit submit. We sit down with SME owners every quarter who tell us the same thing. Their VAT return went in on time, so they assume they're fine. Then the FTA sends a query, or worse, an audit, and it turns out on time was never the same thing as correct. Here is what actually gets checked, and what we look at before any return leaves our hands. 1. Whether the input tax being claimed is actually claimable. Not every expense with VAT on it qualifies. We've seen returns where input tax was claimed on things that should never have been included, and the owner had no idea. 2. Whether the supporting documents match the numbers. A VAT return is only as strong as what's behind it. We go through supporting documents line by line, because a well-known FTA portal does not let you go back and fix it quietly once it's submitted. 3. Whether the output tax reflects what actually happened, not what the bookkeeping software assumed. Systems make mistakes. Second eyes catch them before the FTA does. I think most SME owners are not being reckless. They're just trusting a return they were never shown. We think like owners, so we check the return the way we'd want ours checked, before it goes anywhere near the FTA. Save this for your next VAT filing check.
