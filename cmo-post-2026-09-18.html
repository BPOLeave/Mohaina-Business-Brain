Full Post Text (1,380 characters)

A client of ours had AED 340,000 sitting in the bank and was still losing money every single month. He thought the business was fine because the balance kept growing. It was growing because of a large client deposit he was supposed to return the next quarter, not because the business was actually earning. I think most owners read their bank balance the way other people read a scoreboard. It feels like the whole story. It is not. There are three numbers a bank balance will never show you. 1. Gross margin by service line. Not total revenue, the number that tells you which part of the business is actually making you money and which part is quietly eating it every month. 2. Accounts receivable ageing. Money invoiced is not money collected. We have seen owners plan spending around invoices that were never going to get paid on time, or at all. 3. Committed but unspent liabilities. Deposits, refundable amounts, VAT you are holding for the FTA. It sits in your account, but it was never yours to spend in the first place. We think like owners, so when we build a client's monthly reports, these three sit on the first page, not buried in an appendix nobody opens. What the numbers are telling them matters more than what the balance is showing them. Save this for your next board or bank conversation, before you quote a number that was never actually yours to quote.
