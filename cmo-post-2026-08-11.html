A real estate brokerage in Business Bay was booking commission income the day cash hit the account, not the day it was earned. We picked this up during a routine review. Nobody had flagged anything. Nobody thought there was anything to flag. Two quarters had already gone by. Their bookkeeper had been recording revenue on receipt, so on paper it looked simple. Cash in, income booked. But commission is earned when the deal closes, not when the client transfers the money. That gap meant two quarters of CIT taxable profit were sitting understated, quietly, with nobody the wiser. Nobody had done this on purpose. Their accountant had never been trained to separate the two events. He was capable in some ways, but he didn't know what he didn't know, and the owner had no way of checking his work. I think this is the part most owners miss. You can feel compliant and still be wrong, because the person doing your books doesn't know the difference between cash and income. We caught it going through the supporting documents line by line, not just the ledger totals. Second eyes on every file, checking the timing, not just the amount. That is the actual job. At the end of the day, a healthy bank balance tells you nothing about whether your revenue is recognised correctly. Save this for the situation where nobody has ever reviewed how your revenue is timed.
