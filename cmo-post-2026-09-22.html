Full Post

A restaurant owner in Dubai called us three weeks before a bank meeting. His bookkeeper had told him for months the books were handled. We asked to see the supplier invoices anyway. Second eyes. That's something we do on every engagement, not just the difficult ones. The invoices in the accounting system matched everything perfectly. Numbers were clean. Nothing looked wrong on the screen. Then we pulled the actual supplier statements. AED 140,000 in stock purchases had never made it into the books at all. The bookkeeper had been entering what the owner handed her, not what the suppliers had actually billed. I think that's the part people miss. "Handled" doesn't mean someone is doing the work. It means someone is checking the work. He wasn't being careless. He trusted the person doing his books, the way most owners do. But trust isn't a compliance control. A second set of eyes is. We rebuilt three months of purchases against real supplier statements before that bank meeting. Not because the numbers were fraudulent. Because they were wrong, and wrong numbers in front of a bank cost just as much as fraud does. If a client loses a dirham, it's something that also comes from our pocket. That's not a slogan here. It's why we go through the documents ourselves instead of taking someone's word for it. Save this for the situation where your bookkeeper says "it's handled." P.S. "Handled" is not a status. It's a claim. Ask to see the proof behind it.
