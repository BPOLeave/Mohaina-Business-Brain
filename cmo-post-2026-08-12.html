Real estate is now a named DNFBP category on goAML. Most brokers still don't know what that means for them. I think a lot of real estate firms register for goAML and consider the job done. It isn't. Registration is the start of the file, not the end of it. The Ministry of Economy updated its DNFBP guidance on 9 August, naming real estate directly. We've seen this pattern before with VAT and CIT. The first update is awareness. The second is enforcement. Compliance checks rarely start the week a law changes. They start once the regulator decides everyone has had enough time to catch up. Here's what "actually complete" looks like for an AML/CFT file, not just started: A registered goAML account, with the right person named as the compliance officer. A risk assessment written for your actual transactions, signed and dated this year. Customer due diligence records on every deal, not only the big ones. A log of ongoing monitoring, updated as new deals close. If you're missing even one of those four, your file is started. It's not complete. We think like owners, so we tell every real estate client the same thing. A missing AML file doesn't show up until someone asks for it. By then it's too late to build one properly. DM me AML and I'll walk you through what a complete file actually looks like. P.S. Second eyes on your AML file costs you 30 minutes. Not having one costs a lot more.
