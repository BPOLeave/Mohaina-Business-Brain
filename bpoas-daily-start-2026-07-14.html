Today's LinkedIn Post · Tuesday 14 July 2026

Pillar: Inside an Owner-Led Firm
Format: Story Post · Nurturing
Sector: Logistics (anonymised)
Publish: 08:30 UAE
CTA: Tag a founder who needs to see this
A Dubai freight-forwarding owner walked into his bank last month for a loan. The bank asked one question. He did not have the answer — and neither did his books.
The question was simple. What was your net profit margin last year? He had been in business for six years. He had clients across the Gulf. His turnover was strong. He had invoiced millions in freight coordination fees, customs clearance, and last-mile logistics. He knew his business was healthy. But his books had been kept the way most owner-led businesses in Dubai keep their books. Enough to file. Not enough to interrogate. He could not answer the question. The bank did not say no. Banks rarely say no directly. They said they needed audited financials before they could proceed. He came to us six days later. Here is what we found when we looked at his accounts: 1. Three years of mixing personal and business expenses in the same account. Not unusual. Very common. Completely distorting the margin picture. 2. Invoice timing that made his highest-revenue months look like his worst — because he was recording income on payment, not on delivery. 3. A set of receivables that looked fine on the surface and had about AED 190,000 in balances that had been sitting there for over 180 days with no chase log. None of this made his business bad. His business was genuinely good. But on paper, to a bank, it looked unreadable. The loan did not happen before he needed it. It happened four months later. I'm not telling this story to alarm anyone. I'm telling it because this pattern shows up in every industry and every size of business. And it shows up most often in owner-led firms where the owner is very good at the work and has always handled the finances themselves. The question the bank asks is not the hard part. The hard part is having books that can answer it before you need to. Tag a founder who needs to see this.
