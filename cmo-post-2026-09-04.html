I think most business owners look at their P&L and feel like they know where the business stands. They're reading one number. The business has three more it isn't showing them. Here are the numbers your P&L hides: 1. Your real cash position. Profit on paper and cash in hand are different things. If receivables are aging and payables are due this week, the P&L won't tell you. The cash flow statement will. 2. What your inventory is actually worth. Slow-moving stock sits on the P&L at cost. But if it hasn't moved in months, that number is wrong. We've seen clients carry product at full stated value for years. The real figure was far lower. 3. Who is actually paying you. Revenue recognized. Invoices outstanding. A client who looks like a strong account might be 120 days behind on three invoices. The P&L won't separate them. The AR aging report will. The fix isn't a new system. It's reading three reports you probably already have, alongside the one you're already reading: Cash flow statement Inventory valuation schedule AR aging report If a client loses a dirham, it's something that also comes from our pocket. So we make sure they see all of these numbers, not just the one that reads best. Save this for your next board or bank conversation. P.S. A P&L that looks good and a business that is good are not always the same thing.
