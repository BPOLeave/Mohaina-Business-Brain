Full Post · Copy-Ready

I used to think a healthy bank balance meant a healthy business. I don't think that anymore.

We picked up a FinTech client earlier this year who was growing fast, funds moving in and out through a merchant-float holding account with his payment processor. Every time we looked at his bank balance, things looked good. Cash was there. Numbers were up.

But when we went in and checked the supporting documents against the actual dates the money changed hands, we found the gap. The processor was holding funds for weeks before settling them into his account. His bank balance was already showing revenue that hadn't actually landed yet, not for tax purposes. Left as they were, his books would have recognised that revenue in the wrong period for his CIT return.

Nobody had caught it. Not because anyone was careless, but because a bank balance and a properly recognised number are two different things, and most owners only ever look at one of them.

This is the part I think owners miss most. The bank balance tells you what's sitting in the account today. It doesn't tell you when that money actually became yours, and for CIT, that timing matters.

Second eyes on the supporting documents, not just the ledger, are what catch this before a filing does.

Save this for the situation where your bank balance doesn't match what your books actually say.
