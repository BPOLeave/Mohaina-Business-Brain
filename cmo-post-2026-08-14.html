There are three numbers your P&L will never show you, and they are usually the ones deciding whether you make it through the year.
We had a client in construction whose P&L looked fine every month. Profitable on paper, invoices going out, work coming in. But he kept running short on cash right when payroll and supplier payments landed together, and he could never explain why. The P&L wasn't wrong. It was just incomplete. It never showed him the three things actually controlling his cash position. I think this is the part most owners miss. A profit and loss statement tells you what you earned. It does not tell you when you will see that money, or what it really cost you to earn it. The first is receivables aging, not just what clients owe, but how long it has sat unpaid. Thirty days is a different business than ninety. The second is real margin after cost allocation, what is left once every project cost is actually charged against the invoice, not the headline margin. The third is cash conversion timing, the gap between paying suppliers and getting paid, which decides whether growth funds itself or drains you. Once we built these three into his monthly reporting, the surprises stopped. Not because the business changed. Because he could finally see what the numbers were telling him. At the end of the day, a bank balance tells you where you stand today. It cannot tell you why. Save this for your next board or bank conversation.
