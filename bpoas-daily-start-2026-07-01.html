Invisibility Diagnostic: 4 / 4 PASS PENDING APPROVAL
3 months sounds like a lot. It isn't. Your CIT return is due 30 September. In June, that date feels manageable. By August, when you are still chasing missing invoices, trying to reconcile two years of accounts, and working out what transfer pricing documentation actually requires, it won't. I think most UAE SME owners believe being CIT-ready means submitting a return before the deadline. It doesn't. Readiness is built into the books months before the filing date. Here is what actually needs to be in place before 30 September. Clean, reconciled books for your full first tax year. Not a bank statement print. A complete set of financials you can defend line by line if the FTA asks. A confirmed transfer pricing position. If you transact with related parties or connected entities, the UAE CT Law requires documentation. Most SMEs have not prepared this. Free zone substance confirmed. If you are claiming the 0% qualifying income rate under Article 18, you must pass the substance test. Not assume it. A tax group decision if you have multiple entities. Article 40 group taxation changes what you file and how. A reviewed, finalised return. Not a draft. The businesses that reach September ready started this work in Q2. If you are reading this in June and still unsure where your books stand, that is the signal. Save this for your next finance review.
Pinned Comments (post in order after publishing):
Comment 1 · UAE Law Extension
Under Article 53 of the UAE Corporate Tax Law, the FTA can request supporting records for up to 7 years after a return has been filed. That means your first CIT filing needs to be supported by documentation going back to the first day of your first tax period — not just the invoices you pull together in September. If your books were not properly maintained from day one, that is a reconstruction project, not a filing project. The earlier you identify this, the cheaper it is to fix.
