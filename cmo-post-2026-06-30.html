She called us in month 11 of Year 1 CT. She thought the books were fine.
We said: can we come in tomorrow? She said yes. We arrived at her trading warehouse. She had a full-time accountant. Receipts filed in folders. VAT returns submitted since 2019. Everything looked organised from the outside. Then we opened the ledger. Fixed assets — trucks, equipment, racking — had been expensed in the year of purchase. Not capitalised. Not depreciated. Her CIT computation was going to look very different from what she expected. Supplier invoices from 3 GCC vendors: not FTA-compliant. Could not support the expenses under CIT. Related-party transactions with the owner's second business: undocumented. No transfer pricing analysis. No arm's length basis. VAT returns for 2024: two quarters with input tax claimed on items that were not claimable. Exposure sitting there. Unnoticed. She was not negligent. She was trusting someone who did not know what they did not know. We started that week. Reconstruction, documentation, CIT computation, VAT exposure review. By the time the return was filed, she knew exactly where she stood. Month 11 was late. It was not too late. If you are in month 8, 9, or 10 of Year 1 CT and you have not had a second set of eyes on your books — this is the sign to get them in. What the numbers are telling you and what you think they are saying can be two very different things. Tag a founder who needs to see this.
