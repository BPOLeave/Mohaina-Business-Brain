Today's LinkedIn Post · Copy-Ready
Pillar: What the Numbers Are Telling You · Hook: Curiosity Gap · 4/4 Invisibility Diagnostic · 1,485 chars
Your P&L can look perfectly fine and your business can still be in trouble. Here are the three numbers it hides. I think most owners read one number and stop there. Bank balance up, they relax. Bank balance down, they panic. But the bank balance is not the business. It's just where things stand on one day. The real picture lives somewhere else. Three numbers a clean P&L won't show you on its own. Receivables aging. Money you've invoiced is not money you have. I've seen owners feel proud of a strong sales month while thirty, sixty, ninety days of invoices sit uncollected. The P&L already booked that income. Your bank account is still waiting on it. Real margin after cost allocation. A P&L can show healthy revenue and still hide a line that's losing money once you allocate its true cost, not just the direct cost. We've seen one "profitable" line quietly funded by another. Cash conversion timing. How long it takes from spending the dirham to collecting it back. Two businesses with identical profit on paper can be in very different positions depending on this number alone. We think like owners because we've seen what happens when this goes unchecked. It's rarely fraud. It's usually numbers that were never built to tell the full story alone. Save this for month-end review. Pull your aging report, margin by line, and cash conversion cycle before you trust the P&L again. P.S. A P&L tells you what happened. These three numbers tell you what's actually happening.
