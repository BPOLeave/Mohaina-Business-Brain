What the Numbers Are Telling You Framework List Educating Rec. publish: 08:30 UAE
Your bank balance is not your business health. Here are the three numbers that actually are.I think most SME owners in Dubai check one thing when they want to know how the business is doing. They open their banking app. And I understand it. It is fast. It is visible. It feels real. But your bank balance tells you how much cash you have right now. It does not tell you whether that cash will still be there in 60 days. Three ratios will. Current Ratio (Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities)This tells you whether you can cover what you owe in the next 12 months. Below 1.0 means your short-term liabilities are bigger than your short-term assets. Between 1.5 and 2.0 is generally healthy for a UAE SME. Most owners do not know this number. Debtor Days (Receivables ÷ Revenue × 365)This tells you how long your customers are actually taking to pay. If your payment terms are 30 days but your debtor days are 90, the money is yours on paper. In practice it is sitting in someone else's account. Cash flow problems usually start here, not at the revenue line. Gross Margin (Gross Profit ÷ Revenue × 100)This tells you what is left after paying the direct cost of delivering your product or service. If your margin is shrinking while revenue is growing, you are scaling the problem, not the business. None of these take long to calculate. Most of the time, the reason an owner does not know them is that no one has ever put them in front of the owner in plain language. That is what the numbers are telling you. Save this for your next finance review.

1 · Extend
Current ratio benchmarks vary by industry. Retail typically runs lower because inventory turns faster. Service businesses can run higher with little physical stock. What matters most is the trend across three or four quarters. A falling ratio that is still above 1.0 is worth watching. One that crosses below 1.0 and keeps falling is the conversation you want to have before it becomes urgent.
2 · Humanise (Mohaina)
I think about how many times we have asked an owner: do you know your debtor days? Most of the time, the honest answer is no. Not because they are not across their business. Because no one has ever pulled that number and shown it to them. We ran it for a client during onboarding recently. He looked at the screen and said, so they are actually taking nearly four months to pay me? Yes. That is the moment things start to change.
